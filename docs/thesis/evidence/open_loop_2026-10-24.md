# Evidence: latency at fixed offered rates, final build (2026-10-24)

Script: `bench/open_loop.sh` (`RUNS=3`), unchanged since the Week 5 gate; table by
`tools/open_loop_table.py results/2026-10-24_open_loop/summary.txt`.
- **Load:** open loop at a fixed rate, latency from each call's scheduled send time, so a slow
  server cannot hide queueing (no coordinated omission); 12 channels, 2 s of warm-up, 15 s.
- **Setup:** one limiter (engine `leased`, defaults), not pinned; the development cluster, flushed and
  seeded with 10,000 plans before every run.
- **Health of the runs:** in every one the offered rate was sent in full, with no failed and no
  unanswered call, and the generator's own send lag stayed at most 0.51 ms at p99, so the latencies
  are the server's.

## Reading

- **The knee moved.** At the Week 5 gate, a leased check queued beyond 20,000 checks/s offered,
  reaching p99 of 156 ms at 30,000. Now p99 stays at 3.3 ms at 40,000. That is consistent with the
  throughput gained since: 72,097 to 89,743 checks/s in closed loop, mostly from switching off abseil's
  deadlock detection. Each change was measured on its own in closed loop
  (`verification_and_speedups_2026-10-23.md`); this series was not split by change.
- **The customer check's p99 fell 2.4 to 3.2 times** at 10,000 and 20,000 checks/s.
- **The p999 values are uneven between rates** (16.4 ms at 20,000, but 4.5 ms at 30,000). They come
  from one or two stalls per run on an unpinned laptop, not from a trend.
