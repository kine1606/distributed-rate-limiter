# Evidence: the P3 gate, billing under a killed master and capacity under a Free flood (2026-10-25)

The P3 gate of `docs/PRODUCT_PLAN.md`, in two parts:
- **Billing:** a month of postpaid traffic with retries and a killed master, where invoices equal the
  counters, any difference reported.
- **Capacity:** the merchant capacity never exceeded beyond N × C, and paying customers keep their
  reserve under a Free flood.

Code: the working tree on top of commit `bd24b60` (branch `feat/merchants`), not committed when
measured. Laptop: the load, two limiters and Redis share 12 hardware threads.

## 1. Billing with a master killed

Script: `tests/eval/billing_gate.py` (`control/.venv/bin/python`). Raw output:
`results/2026-10-25_billing_gate/`, one `summary_*.json` per run.

**Setup.**
- *Cluster:* the fault cluster (`docker/docker-compose.faults.yml`): 3 masters with one replica each,
  AOF with `appendfsync everysec`, failover after about 2 to 3 s.
- *Limiters:* two (`RL_TENANCY=merchants`, engine `leased`), usage events on.
- *Customers:* one merchant with 40 on a postpaid plan. Each has 150 calls a month within the plan,
  then billed overage up to a cap far above the load. The plan fails closed, so no call is admitted
  without a Redis check, and charges a fee of $19.00 and $0.50 per 1,000 overage calls.
- *Load:* 1,500 calls a second for 20 s over both limiters, each call with its own request id.
  - *Retries:* a call is retried with the same id when the RPC fails or the answer is `UNAVAILABLE`,
    up to 6 attempts (backoff 0.2 s, 0.4 s, and so on), as a gateway would.
  - *The kill:* 8 s in, the master holding the first customer's counter is killed (`docker kill`).
    After the run it is started again and rejoins as a replica.

**Comparison.** For each customer, four numbers:
1. *The gateway's view:* what it was told, as calls within the plan and billed overage.
2. *The counters:* the month's totals that `lua/quota.lua` keeps (`bu`, `bo`).
3. *The invoice:* made from those counters by `control/rlcontrol/billing.py`. The month cannot end
   during a test, so the invoice is this month's `billing.preview`, built by the same function that
   closes a month. Closing an ended month is tested separately in `control/tests/test_billing.py`.
4. *The usage events:* both limiters' records of their decisions.

| Run | Master killed | Calls | Told: in plan / overage | No decision after 6 tries | Counters: in plan / overage | Invoice overage, total | Events: in plan / overage | Customers where the counter differs from the gateway | where the invoice differs from the counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no kill | none | 29,994 | 6,000 / 23,994 | 0 | 6,000 / 23,994 | 23,994, $772.01 | 6,000 / 23,994 | 0 | 0 |
| kill 1 | 7106 | 29,998 | 6,000 / 23,196 | 802 | 6,000 / 23,196 | 23,196, $771.58 | 6,000 / 23,192 | 0 | 0 |
| kill 2 | 7103 | 29,996 | 6,000 / 23,526 | 470 | 6,000 / 23,526 | 23,526, $771.80 | 6,000 / 23,526 | 0 | 0 |
| kill 3 | 7105 | 29,996 | 6,000 / 23,347 | 649 | 6,000 / 23,347 | 23,347, $771.71 | 6,000 / 23,344 | 0 | 0 |
| kill 4 | 7104 | 29,993 | 6,000 / 23,474 | 519 | 6,000 / 23,474 | 23,474, $771.75 | 6,000 / 23,457 | 0 | 0 |

- **Invoices equal the counters, and the counters equal what the gateway was told,** for every customer
  in every run, kills included. Every customer used its 150 calls within the plan (6,000 in all).
  - *The totals:* 40 fees of $19.00 make $760.00. The overage makes the rest, rounded per invoice:
    23,994 calls at $0.50 per 1,000 is $12.00 before rounding, and 40 invoices rounded half up make
    $12.01.
- **No increment was lost to a failover in these four kills.**
  - *What could still lose one:* replication is asynchronous, so a master killed between acknowledging
    a quota write and passing it on would lose that write. The counter would then be lower than the
    gateway's view. It did not happen in these runs.
  - *Why it was not prevented:* that would need a `WAIT` for the replica on every quota write, which
    the design rejects (3.9.6). The gate's job is to report such a difference, not to hide it.
- **Calls with no decision were neither admitted nor billed.** In each kill run, 470 to 802 calls got no
  decision within the gateway's 6 tries, while the replica took over. The gateway was never told they
  were admitted, and no counter counted them.
- **The usage events are short; the counters are not.**
  - *How much:* in three of the four kill runs, the events recorded 3 to 17 fewer overage calls than
    the counters, against 0 with no kill.
  - *Why:* a quota script ran and counted, but its reply missed the limiter's 50 ms deadline during
    the failover. The limiter answered `UNAVAILABLE` and recorded an "unavailable" event. The gateway
    retried with the same id and got the stored answer, which is never recorded again (3.4.3). The
    call is in the counter and on the invoice, but its event says "unavailable".
  - *Checked:* in kill 4, for each of the 9 customers concerned, the missing overage events equal its
    "unavailable" events.
  - *What it shows:* why billing reads the counters, not the events (3.9.6). `python -m
    rlcontrol.billing reconcile` reports this kind of difference.

## 2. Capacity under a Free flood

`Property.MerchantCapacityHoldsAndPayingCustomersKeepTheReserve` (`tests/property_test.cc`),
`RL_PROPERTY_SEEDS=2000`.

**Per seed:**
- *Instances:* 2 to 4 limiter engines (CompositeCore over LeasedCore) share one fake Redis that runs the
  scripts' rules on a fake clock.
- *The merchant:* a capacity of 100, 200 or 500 calls a second, a burst of 1 to 2 seconds of it, and
  a reserve of 10%, 20% or 30%. The limits are built from these terms by `MerchantKeys`, as the server
  loads them.
- *The load, for 4 s in 1 ms ticks:* five Free customers at 3 times the capacity, and two paying
  customers (a plan with a fee) at 70% of the reserve's rate.

**Checks, for every window of 1, 10, 100, 1,000 and 4,000 ms:**
- *All calls:* admitted ≤ burst + rate × window + N × (C<sub>shared</sub> + C<sub>reserve</sub>), each
  C being its bucket's chunk cap.
- *Free calls alone:* admitted ≤ the shared part's burst + its rate × window + N × C<sub>shared</sub>.
  More would mean Free calls reached the reserve.
- *Paying customers:* they get at least 98% of what they ask.

| Seeds | Calls admitted | Fullest window, share of its bound | Free calls refused | Paying customers: kept / asked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2,000 | 2,520,961 | 91% | 4,175,504 | 303,203 / 303,203 |

**What this shows.**
- *The capacity held:* no window over the bound in 2,000 seeds; the fullest reached 91% of it.
- *The reserve held:* no Free call reached it.
- *Paying customers lost nothing:* they kept every call they asked for, while Free demand ran at three
  times the capacity.
- *The real limiter:* `control/tests/test_e2e.py` repeats the essentials with one limiter on the dev
  cluster.
  - *Capacity:* 10 calls a second with half reserved. A Free customer got 5 or 6 of 20 rapid calls; a
    paying customer then got all 5 from the reserve.
  - *Check rate:* 20 a second; 20 to 26 of 40 rapid checks were admitted, the rest refused.

**Proof that the checks can fail.** With a Free plan allowed into the reserve, seed 1 fails: "Free took
177 in 1000 ms, its share is 176: the reserve leaked". With the capacity switched off, the same seed
fails the same way. Losing the reserve in the split crashes the harness. A lease that refills past one
chunk is not caught here, because this bound sums both buckets' lease slack. The leased-quota harness
catches it (seed 22).
