# Evidence: Week 5 gate — a Redis master killed under load, and latency at fixed rates (2026-10-21)

The gate: "kill a Redis master during load and observe the documented behaviour per plan; latency
at a fixed rate is measured open loop." Both parts are below, with three design faults the fault test
found and how each was fixed.

## A. A master killed under load

**Setup.** A fault cluster of 3 masters with one replica each (`docker/docker-compose.faults.yml`,
`cluster-node-timeout 2000`, no persistence), one limiter (engine `leased`, defaults), and three
open-loop load generators for 30 s after 2 s of warm-up (`bench/fault_kill_master.sh`):

| Stream | Rule | Customers | Offered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| free | `customer` | `cust:1..1000`, Free (fails closed) | 1,000 checks/s |
| pay | `customer` | `cust:pay:1..1000`, Pay (fails open) | 5,000 checks/s |
| lease | `api_default` (token bucket served from leases, fails open) | `cust:1..1000` | 10,000 checks/s |

Ten seconds in, the master owning slot 8,000 (a third of the slots) is killed with `docker kill`
(no clean shutdown). Its replica is promoted about 2 to 3 s later.

**Documented behaviour** (DESIGN.md section 8): leases serve while they hold tokens; then each plan's
failure policy applies to the keys whose master is gone: Pay is admitted as `UNVERIFIED` (not
counted, not billed), Free is denied `UNAVAILABLE`; everything returns to normal after the failover.

**Result, final design** (3 runs, per-slot-group breakers, `cluster-require-full-coverage no`):

| Run | Free: `UNAVAILABLE` | Pay: `UNVERIFIED` | Lease: `UNVERIFIED` | Degraded seconds | Errors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1,442 | 7,113 | 519 | 10 to 14 | 0 |
| 2 | 1,386 | 6,810 | 421 | 10 to 14 | 0 |
| 3 | 1,095 | 5,417 | 48 | 10 to 13 | 0 |

Run 2 second by second (`ok / unverified / unavailable`):

| Second | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| free | 911/0/0 | 623/0/333 | 589/0/339 | 607/0/333 | 621/0/321 | 868/0/60 | 896/0/0 |
| pay | 5000/0/0 | 3406/1594/0 | 3310/1690/0 | 3308/1692/0 | 3413/1587/0 | 4753/247/0 | 5000/0/0 |
| lease | 10000/0/0 | 10000/0/0 | 10000/0/0 | 9984/16/0 | 9707/293/0 | 9888/112/0 | 10000/0/0 |

- **Exactly the dead master's share.** During the outage one third of the Free checks were denied and
  one third of the Pay checks admitted unverified (333 of about 1,000 and about 1,600 of 5,000 a
  second); customers on the two healthy masters saw nothing. No Pay check was denied, no Free check
  was admitted unverified, and no call failed.
- **Leases bought two seconds.** The leased rule kept answering from leases until second 12, and only
  keys whose lease then ran dry fell back to the rule's policy.
- **Recovery in 4 to 5 s** from the kill: about 2 s for the cluster to declare the master failed, the
  promotion, then the next call to those slots is re-routed to the new master.

**What the test found on the way** (one run per design before the fix, `results/2026-10-21_fault_kill_master/`):

| Design | Free `UNAVAILABLE` | Pay `UNVERIFIED` | Lease `UNVERIFIED` | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One breaker for the whole cluster | 4,735 | 23,573 | 1,434 | When the master died, every call pending on its connections failed at once: 20 failures in a row opened the breaker, six times, for every customer. Seconds 11 to 13: 976 to 998 of 1,000 Free checks denied. |
| Breaker per group of 256 slots, Redis default `cluster-require-full-coverage yes` (3 runs) | 1,716 to 1,792 | 8,541 to 8,873 | 216 to 245 | From the moment the master was marked failed until the promotion, every node answered `CLUSTERDOWN`, for every slot (logged), so for about a second all customers fell back to their policy. |
| Breaker per slot group, `cluster-require-full-coverage no` (3 runs, above) | 1,095 to 1,442 | 5,417 to 7,113 | 48 to 519 | Only the dead master's slots. |

A third fault was found by reading the code before the run: scripts went straight to the node the
client's slot map named and did not follow a failover. A script that meets `MOVED` or a lost node is
now sent once more after a routed command refreshes the map (not a quota script after a lost
connection, which may have run): `rl_redis_reroutes_total` counted 989 of them in run 2.

Recommendation recorded in DESIGN.md: run the Redis Cluster with `cluster-require-full-coverage no`,
so a failover costs only the failed master's slots.

## B. Latency at fixed rates, measured open loop

**Setup.** `bench/open_loop.sh`: one limiter (engine `leased`, defaults) on the development cluster,
re-seeded before each run; `bench/loadgen` in open-loop mode (calls on a fixed schedule over 12
connections, latency from each call's scheduled time, the generator's own send lag reported), 2 s of
warm-up and 15 s measured, 3 runs per rate. A closed-loop generator would hide stalls: freezing the
server for 0.5 s at 10,000 calls/s showed a p99 of 480.8 ms open loop and 5.8 ms closed loop.

Medians of 3 runs (p99 range in brackets). Every run sent its full rate: no call skipped, none
failed. Send lag is how late the generator's own sends were (p99), to show it kept up.

| Rule | Offered | p50 | p99 | p999 | Send lag p99 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `api_default` (leased) | 10,000/s | 0.29 ms | **0.83 ms** (0.79 to 0.94) | 1.96 ms | 0.13 ms |
| `api_default` (leased) | 20,000/s | 0.64 ms | **1.80 ms** (1.72 to 1.88) | 3.03 ms | 0.19 ms |
| `api_default` (leased) | 30,000/s | 1.96 ms | 155.9 ms (131.1 to 220.4) | 220.4 ms | 0.49 ms |
| `api_default` (leased) | 40,000/s | 2.66 ms | 136.9 ms (115.1 to 185.4) | 185.4 ms | 0.79 ms |
| `customer` (Pay customers) | 5,000/s | 1.17 ms | 2.14 ms (2.14 to 2.23) | 3.16 ms | 0.17 ms |
| `customer` (Pay customers) | 10,000/s | 1.80 ms | 8.19 ms (4.87 to 28.77) | 15.69 ms | 0.56 ms |
| `customer` (Pay customers) | 20,000/s | 3.92 ms | 19.48 ms (9.33 to 34.22) | 27.55 ms | 1.17 ms |

- **A leased check meets the Week 3 target at 10,000/s with room to spare** (p99 0.83 ms against
  2 ms), measured honestly now; the Week 3 attempt with `ghz --rps` gave 2.20 ms.
- **The knee on this laptop is between 20,000 and 30,000 checks/s offered** for single-check calls,
  with client and limiter on the same 12 hardware threads. Past it, calls queue (p50 stays near 2 ms,
  p99 rises to 130 to 220 ms) instead of failing.
- **The cap also helped below the knee**: at 10,000/s the uncapped series had a p99 of 2.33 ms.
- **A customer check** costs a Redis call for the burst limit, and its p99 grows with the rate: 2.1 ms
  at 5,000/s, 19.5 ms at 20,000/s.

**What open loop found.** In a first series without a cap on concurrent calls per connection (its
summary file was overwritten by the series above; the numbers are from its printed summary), the
rates of 30,000 and 40,000 checks/s collapsed in every run: once calls queued, thousands of streams
per connection made each call cost more, the backlog never drained, and most calls missed their 2 s
deadline (30,000/s: 15,066 calls a second sent, 418,514 failed). The same load on a server with no
limiter at all (`allow_all`) collapsed the same way, so it is gRPC on a laptop shared by client and
server, not the limiter. Capping concurrent calls at 100 per connection (`RL_MAX_STREAMS`, now the
default) turned the collapse into queueing that drains (`allow_all` at 30,000/s: p99 2,097 ms
uncapped, 137 ms at 100, 101 ms at 32). Its cost: one client connection with 192 calls in flight on
the exact path does 22,957 instead of 28,251 checks/s; a gateway that needs more opens more
connections or uses `CheckBatch`.

## Gate status

Met: the documented behaviour per plan is observed with a master killed under load, and latency at
fixed rates is measured open loop. Limits: one laptop for client, limiter and Redis; one limiter
instance; three runs per cell.
