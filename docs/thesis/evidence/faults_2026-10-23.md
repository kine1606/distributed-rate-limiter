# Evidence: fault tests: slow, silent and killed masters, dropped connections (evaluation test 9, 2026-10-23)

Week 6 asks for "kill a master, add 20 ms latency, drop connections; record allowed, denied and
unverified counts and recovery time per plan". The test also found a fault in the design, which was
fixed and measured again.

## Setup

- **Script:** `tests/eval/faults.sh` (`RUNS=3`), on the development cluster (3 masters). The killed
  master is `bench/fault_kill_master.sh` (`RUNS=3`) on the fault cluster (3 masters with one replica
  each).
- **Limiter:** one instance, engine `leased`, defaults: a Redis timeout of 50 ms, 4 Redis clients,
  breakers per slot group.
- **Load:** three open-loop streams, as in the Week 5 gate:

  | Stream | Rule | Customers | Rate |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | free | `customer` | `cust:1..1000`, Free: fails closed | 1,000 checks/s |
  | pay | `customer` | `cust:pay:1..1000`, Pay: fails open | 5,000 checks/s |
  | lease | `api_default`, served from leases | `cust:1..1000` | 10,000 checks/s |

- **Phases:** each fault is measured in three phases of 10 s, each after 1 s of warm-up: before,
  during and after.
- **Faults:** latency and loss come from `tc netem` in the network namespace the masters share; only
  their replies to the limiter pass through it, not the cluster's own traffic.
  - *latency20*: +20 ms on every master, below the 50 ms timeout.
  - *latency80*: +80 ms on one master, above it.
  - *silent*: every reply of one master lost, a partition that sends no reset.
  - *drops*: every 2 s, `CLIENT KILL TYPE normal` on every master (5 times), and once
    `CLIENT KILL TYPE pubsub`.
- **Machine:** laptop, i5-12450H, WSL2, Redis 7.4.11 in Docker.

## A finding: the 50 ms timeout did not bound a call

The first run of *latency80*, before the fix, gave a master that answered every call 80 ms late. Its
customers were **not** given their plan's failure policy: every check succeeded, slowly.

| One master +80 ms, before the fix (1 run) | p50 | p99 | Policy answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| free | 1.88 ms | **251 ms** | 0 |
| pay | 1.96 ms | **251 ms** | 0 |
| lease | 0.64 ms | 5.5 ms | 0 |

The client library's timeout (hiredis, through redis-plus-plus) is an idle timeout per connection,
re-armed by any data that arrives. Under steady traffic, a slow master's connection is never idle
for 50 ms, so no call timed out. A customer check on that master waited for three slow round trips
(plan read, burst, quota), about 250 ms. A master slower still would have pushed checks past their
callers' deadlines, holding a slot of the in-flight limit all the while. The code comment that said
the timeout "also bounds each async command" was wrong.

**The fix.** `ResilientBackend` now gives every attempt a deadline (`RetryOptions::deadline`, set to
the Redis timeout in `main.cc`). Whichever comes first answers: the reply, or the deadline as a
timeout. The other is dropped, and a late reply never counts as a success for the breaker. A missed
deadline is a timeout like any other: it feeds the slot group's breaker and is retried where a retry
cannot loosen a limit. Deadlines wait in one queue in send order, and the existing timer thread
checks them in batches every 5 ms or more, so it does not wake for every call. Missed deadlines are
counted in `rl_redis_deadline_misses_total`. Six unit tests cover the new behaviour (`tests/circuit_breaker_test.cc`,
`ResilientDeadline.*`).

**Its cost**, measured with the old and new servers alternating, 5 runs each, closed loop (12
connections × 16 calls in flight, 10,000 customers):

| Workload | Before | After | Deadlines missed after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| exact daily quota (engine `strict`) | 43,281 checks/s (40,493 to 43,803) | 41,820 (39,987 to 43,174), −3.4% | 0 in every run |
| customer rule (engine `leased`) | 37,753 checks/s (35,484 to 38,534) | 35,930 (34,462 to 37,770), −4.8% | 0 in every run |

A lease hit does not call Redis, so the leased fast path pays nothing. The risk of a per-call
deadline is false timeouts under overload, so the customer rule was also run open loop past its knee
for 15 s:

| Offered | Build | p50 | p99 | Deadlines missed | Fail-open answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20,000/s | before | 3.44 ms | 17.1 ms | (no counter) | 0 |
| 20,000/s | after | 3.30 ms | 15.0 ms | 0 | 0 |
| 30,000/s | before | 5.55 ms | 28.8 ms | (no counter) | 0 |
| 30,000/s | after | 6.05 ms | 34.2 ms | 0 | 0 |

## Results with the fix

Medians of 3 runs. Every call was sent on schedule (none skipped). "Policy answers" are the plan's
failure policy: `UNAVAILABLE` for Free, `UNVERIFIED` for Pay and for the leased rule, which fails open.

**+20 ms on every master (below the timeout).** Nothing failed; the latency of each kind of check
shows what it costs.

| Stream | Before: p50 / p99 | During: p50 / p99 | After: p50 / p99 | Policy answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| free | 1.45 / 3.92 ms | **42.5 / 65.5 ms** | 1.51 / 4.87 ms | 0 |
| pay | 1.45 / 3.92 ms | **42.5 / 62.8 ms** | 1.51 / 4.66 ms | 0 |
| lease | 0.61 / 2.23 ms | **0.64 / 2.05 ms** | 0.64 / 2.77 ms | 0 |

A customer check waits for two round trips, burst then quota, so +20 ms on Redis is +40 ms on the
check. A leased check does not notice: its refills start at 30% of the chunk, well before a key runs
dry. This is the case for leases in one line.

**+80 ms on one master (above the timeout), and one master silent.** Both behave the same way, as
the design says a slow or dead master should.

| Fault | Stream | During: answers by policy, of all | During p99 | After: policy answers | Recovered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +80 ms | free | 3,267 of 10,000 `UNAVAILABLE` | 3.60 ms | 39 | within 1 s |
| +80 ms | pay | 17,288 of 50,000 `UNVERIFIED` | 3.44 ms | 197 | within 1 s |
| +80 ms | lease | 15,451 of 100,000 `UNVERIFIED` | 1.80 ms | 372 | within 1 s |
| silent | free | 3,265 of 10,000 `UNAVAILABLE` | 4.10 ms | 0 | before the next phase |
| silent | pay | 17,288 of 50,000 `UNVERIFIED` | 3.92 ms | 0 | before the next phase |
| silent | lease | 15,458 of 100,000 `UNVERIFIED` | 1.65 ms | 0 | before the next phase |

- **Exactly the slow master's third.** About 330 Free and 1,730 Pay answers a second followed the
  policy, from the first second measured: the breakers of that master's slot groups had already opened,
  so those calls failed at once (p99 under 4 ms). Customers on the other two masters saw nothing.
- **Leases bought time.** The leased rule's policy answers rose from 2 to about 3,200 a second over
  the 10 s, as one lease after another ran dry and could not be refilled. Every other key kept being
  served from its lease.
- **Recovery** after the delay was removed: the breakers' probes closed them within the first
  second; the server log shows "all closed again".
- No call failed in any of these runs.

**Dropped connections.** Five kills of every Redis connection over 10 s, and one kill of the
plan-change subscription:

| Stream | During: policy answers (runs 1, 2, 3) | Errors | p99 during |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| free | 2, 3, 2 | 0 | 4.10 ms |
| pay | 11, 18, 17 | 0 | 3.92 ms |
| lease | 0, 0, 0 | 0 | 2.14 ms |

Only the calls in flight at the moment of a kill fell back to the policy, one to six per kill. The
clients reconnected at once and no lease went dry. The plan-change subscription was back 0.5 s after
its kill in every run (server log: "subscription lost, retrying every 500 ms", then "subscribed"); on
reconnection the listener drops every cached plan, because messages may have been lost meanwhile.

**A master killed** (`docker kill`, its replica promoted, fault cluster, this build):

| Run | Free `UNAVAILABLE` | Pay `UNVERIFIED` | Lease `UNVERIFIED` | Degraded seconds | Errors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1,148 | 5,667 | 67 | 9 to 13 | 0 |
| 2 | 1,874 | 9,261 | 503 | 10 to 14 | 0 |
| 3 | 1,279 | 6,402 | 258 | 10 to 14 | 0 |

These match the Week 5 gate (`week5_gate_2026-10-21.md`): a third of the customers get their policy
for 4 to 5 s, and no call fails.

## One unexplained blip

In 1 of 8 runs of *latency20*, 9 Pay calls (0.02% of that phase) failed at the client, in the first
second after the delay was removed. The load generator gives a call 2 s, so these went unanswered for
that long; the limiter logged nothing. Five more runs did not reproduce it. The load generator now
records the gRPC status of failed calls (`error_codes`), so a repeat will say which status it was.
The cause is not identified.

## Limits

- One limiter, one laptop; latency injected on the Redis side only.
- `tc netem` adds a fixed delay with no jitter, and it drops queued packets when it is removed.
- Raw output: `results/2026-10-23_faults/`, `results/2026-10-23_fault_kill_master/` (git-ignored).
