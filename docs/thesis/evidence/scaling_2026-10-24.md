# Evidence: one instance under rising concurrency, the USL and Little's law (Week 8, 2026-10-24)

Script: `bench/concurrency_sweep.sh` (`RUNS=3`, 90 runs, no failed call).
- **Load:** closed loop at N = 1 to 384 calls in flight, with one channel per call up to 12 and 12
  channels beyond.
- **Engines:** gRPC alone; the strict engine over Zipf 1M keys; the leased engine over Zipf 10k keys
  (mostly lease hits). One instance, pinned as in the matrix.
- **Analysis:** `tools/usl_fit.py results/2026-10-24_concurrency/summary.txt docs/thesis/figures/usl.png`.

## Universal Scalability Law

X(N) = λN / (1 + σ(N − 1) + κN(N − 1)), fitted by least squares to every run:

| Engine | lambda (fit) | X(1) measured | sigma | kappa | R² | median abs. rel. error | Peak N* | Ceiling (fit) | Max measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gRPC alone | 2,585 | 8,228 | 0.0222 | 9.5e-24 | 0.976 | 12% | none (kappa about 0) | 116,258 | 105,626 |
| strict (Redis per check) | 1,056 | 2,910 | 0.0151 | 1.7e-19 | 0.982 | 18% | none (kappa about 0) | 69,924 | 62,510 |
| leased (lease hits) | 2,449 | 7,258 | 0.0208 | 1.4e-18 | 0.985 | 10% | none (kappa about 0) | 117,771 | 104,581 |

![Throughput against concurrency](../figures/usl.png)

**The USL does not describe one instance's curve.**
- **Least squares** fits the plateau (R² 0.98) but puts λ, the rate at one call in flight, at a third
  of the measured value (2,585 against 8,228 for gRPC alone).
- **A fit on relative error** does the reverse: it misses the plateau by up to 49% (median error 16 to
  21%), and fitting only N ≤ 12 gives different parameters again.
- **The likely reason:** the USL assumes each request costs the same at any concurrency, but here
  the cost per call falls as calls pile up. gRPC's HTTP/2 transport and the Redis client both send
  more calls per write and per wake-up when more are in flight. This was not measured directly.

**What the data shows without the model:**
- *Throughput never falls as concurrency rises,* up to 384 calls in flight: each median is above the
  one before, and κ is about 0 in every fit. So there is no coherency penalty in range, and the
  server's limits on concurrent calls (`RL_MAX_STREAMS`, `RL_MAX_IN_FLIGHT`) are what protects it
  beyond.
- *The leased engine follows gRPC alone at every N* (7,258 against 8,228 at N = 1; 104,581 against
  105,626 at N = 384).
- *The strict engine levels off near 62,500*, where each check waits for a Redis round trip.

For 1,000,000 checks/s, the USL adds nothing to the per-instance figures of `tools/extrapolate.py`.
Scaling across instances needs separate machines to measure.

## Little's law

In a closed loop N = X × R, so N / X is the mean latency.
- **Result:** in all 30 cells, N / X lies between the measured p50 (within 10%) and p99. For example,
  for the leased engine at N = 192, N / X is 1.94 ms against a p50 of 1.96 ms and a p99 of 3.44 ms.
- **What it shows:** the load generator issues the next call as soon as one returns, and its latency
  figures account for the whole time a call is out. They are not missing the client's own delay. The
  full table is in the script's output.
